## Tunes: My Album

For many years, friends and family have heard me mention an "album." Well, for better or worse, this is it—mistakes and all. 

It is a little late now to correct the past, so there is no need to listen *too* carefully—you'll hear plenty of flaws! This is an eclectic collection rather than a polished studio album, spanning over 35 years of playing. It contains primarily original tunes and songs, recovered (quite obviously) from very old recording room test tapes. 

### A Reflection on My Guitar Journey
Almost no one has ever heard me play guitar, let alone heard these tunes, because I rarely played outside my own home. I began teaching myself at age 15. After learning a few songs with close friends, I spent most of my time making up and learning my own compositions. I just never got comfortable performing them for others. 

In an ironic twist of fate, a slowly progressing **Benign Tremor** in both of my hands has finally curtailed my playing for good. But hey, throw in a few clichés like *"Better Late Than Never,"* *"Put Your Money Where Your Mouth Is,"* and *"Fortune Favors the Brave,"* and this particular page and its contents have finally been realized.

### [Category 1: The Early Recordings (AKAI Reel-to-Reel Experiments)]

#### **1. Look Into My Eyes** (3:38)
*   **The Story:** My first-ever love song. I was just 21, working my first real job, and got invited to my boss's house for dinner. There, I fell head-over-heels for his wife. The result was this soul-searching look into the heart of a young boy. 
*   **Technical Note:** I was still learning the ropes on a brand new AKAI reel-to-reel tape deck. You can hear the volume fluctuate during the first 10 seconds, and the overall quality is quite poor. This is likely the oldest original recording of my music that I own (circa 1971).

#### **2. Dual in AM** (1:42)
*   **The Story:** An early overdubbing attempt using my AKAI reel-to-reel tape deck. 
*   **Technical Note:** This deck allowed me to play a rhythm track (which you can hear in the background) and then replay that track twice to overlay the "lead" guitar in the foreground. The deck didn't work exactly as expected—creating an increasing echo with each added track. I ended up returning the AKAI and paying more for a Toshiba, which worked perfectly. It was hours and hours of great fun.

#### **3. Dual in Em** (2:41)
*   **The Story:** A softer overdubbing output and my first experiment in **Open E tuning**. 
*   **Technical Note:** Strumming the guitar without pressing any strings makes a perfect E chord, meaning you are always in tune. You can play lead easily by pressing notes in the E scale, knowing that even if you accidentally strike other strings, it still makes a harmonious sound. 
*   **The Joni Mitchell Encounter:** I learned this open-tuning technique after watching Joni Mitchell play open string a mere 10 feet in front of me at an Ottawa coffee house. If I recall, it cost me about $2 to see her, and there couldn't have been more than 50 seats in the whole place. It was a fantastic, once-in-a-lifetime experience. Unfortunately, we actually left too early that night! *(Click the link below in the page notes to find out why).*

### [Category 2: Reflections & Interpretations]

#### **4. One Day** (4:07)
*   **The Story:** My cover of Murray McLauchlan's *"Revelations."* It became my true personal anthem the day I first heard it, and I played it almost every day since. 
*   **Meaning:** To me, the song was a prophetic message some 60 years ahead of its time, foretelling the embrace of MAID (Medical Assistance in Dying) among the increasing global population of aging peoples.
*   **Technical Note:** I didn't realize I had been playing it slightly wrong for a while and missed recording the first of the two choruses. I guess you can't get any more genuine than that!

#### **5. So Long** (3:17)
*   **The Story:** Another song from my earlier days, featuring a strong rock beat. It turns the usual pop structure upside down by putting the main message inside the chorus and the repeated scolding in the verse. This created a very strong personal "declaration" that I sang frequently when playing alone.
*   **Technical Note:** Oops! I had my harmonica backward in the holder during this take. It caught me by surprise, so I had to finish playing the bar with it backward before flipping it around after the next verse. I originally planned to do a complete retake, but that day never came. Should'a, would'a, could'a!

### [Category 3: Tributes & Later Works]

#### **7. Norma** (3:05)
*   **The Story:** I loved this song, but this particular recording has several issues. It was my first recorded song with just me alone on a single guitar—no overdubbing. I wanted everything to go perfectly, so of course one string misbehaved and kept "tinging" every once in a while. 
*   **Technical Note:** The tune also runs on too long because I originally intended to practice some lead over it. I always planned to re-record it, but that day never came. However, I have played this tune every day in appreciation of my sweetie, so it will always remain a key part of my album.

#### **9. Amsterdam Night** (4:05)
*   **The Story:** A crazy afternoon in Amsterdam found us down by the waterfront on the wrong side of the wharfs as darkness was falling. We were approached by a strange group of about 5 men who had exited a suddenly stopped vehicle. I grabbed my lady (who was entirely oblivious to the danger), turned around, headed back over the pier, and never looked back. This tune mimics the adrenaline-rushing feelings I had during that close encounter.

#### **10. Aunts & Uncles** (3:12)
*   **The Story:** My last recorded effort. I was attempting to develop a piece that mimicked two distinct parts—one representing my Aunts and one for my Uncles. I ran out of time and patience and couldn't complete it exactly as I wanted. Tragically, I never got to play it for them before they passed.
*   **Technical Note:** As time progressed, so did my Benign Tremor. Towards the end of this piece, you can hear the mistakes increasing to what I considered an unacceptable level. It was at this point that I decided to put my guitar away and concentrate my energies elsewhere.
