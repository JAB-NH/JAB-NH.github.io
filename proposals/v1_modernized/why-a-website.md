## Why a Website?

Is it worth the time and effort to maintain a personal website in the 21st century? For me, the answer is a resounding yes. It boils down to four main motivations:

---

### [Collapsible Group: 1. Vulnerability & Commitment]
> **Subtitle:** Standing by your beliefs publicly.
>
> It is one thing to hold a contentious point of view privately. It is another thing to anonymously post a contentious point of view on the internet. But it takes true **vulnerability and commitment** to publicly express a contentious point of view under your own name, where you can be held accountable for it. 
> 
> This site is a commitment to sharing what I believe, openly and honestly.

---

### [Collapsible Group: 2. Future Reference]
> **Subtitle:** A digital legacy for those who come after.
> 
> I have finally reached an age where I would love to know all kinds of things about my parents and grandparents. However, I find that they are all gone, and I missed out on that opportunity.
> 
> This realization got me thinking: perhaps a collection of odds and ends from my own personal experiences might eventually be of some interest to someone else someday. This website is the result of that process. It is a repository for my children, grandchildren, and friends to explore whenever they want to learn a bit more about where they came from.

---

### [Collapsible Group: 3. Entertainment]
> **Subtitle:** The joy of building and experimenting.
> 
> Beyond the personal archives, how much fun has this been? Building this site and experimenting with the web has been an incredible playground. Over the years, I have built interactive games, image recognition systems, and even rudimentary neural networks to garner a basic understanding of how these new technological environments will impact our future.
> 
> It has been an absolute blast, and I know far more now about the future of software and technology than I ever imagined I would.

---

### [Collapsible Group: 4. Demonstration & Education]
> **Subtitle:** Keeping pace with an accelerating digital world.
> 
> The pace of internet learning is exploding. I often feel that as quickly as I learn a new skill, the very next week some new capability is already required. 
> 
> Modern tools have changed the game. Today, creating your own website couldn't be easier. It is all drag-and-drop, with no coding required. Everybody has something worth saving and sharing:
> *   Pictures of favorite meal preps or amateur artwork
> *   Home videos or music files (MP3s)
> *   Unpublished novels, books of poems, or documents (PDFs)
> 
> My hope is to demonstrate that anyone can build a modern web presence. By letting others provide the layout and design frameworks, we can concentrate entirely on the actual content and learn new digital skills along the way.
